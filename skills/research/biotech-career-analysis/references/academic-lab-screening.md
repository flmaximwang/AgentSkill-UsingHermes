# Screening academic labs / postdoc groups

Use when the user evaluates academic positions (postdoc, assistant researcher, junior PI) and has stated
non-negotiables about the advisor and the group. Written for a lab-science user; the tests are advisor
output, run intensity, and how much of the work is thinking rather than hands.

## 0. The tension to surface first

"High advisor output" and "low work intensity" are usually mutually exclusive: high output normally comes
from headcount × hours. The target is therefore not "a productive group" but a **mature, systematized
high-output group** — output carried by infrastructure, technical staff and accumulated work rather than
by student hours. Say this before scoring anything; otherwise the user screens for an impossible profile.

Note also that "low intensity" and "low thought-content" are independent axes: a relaxed group can still
treat a postdoc as an extra pair of hands. Screen them separately.

## 1. Advisor output — verifiable indicators

| Indicator | Where to look | Read |
|------|------|------|
| **Corresponding-author papers in the last 5 years** (not total output) | PubMed / Google Scholar / group publication list | the decisive number; "corresponding" means the PI led it |
| Output trend (last 3 yr vs previous 3 yr) | same | flat/rising = systematized; clear decline = moved to administration or inactive |
| Journal tier and a continuing sequence of representative work | same | a sustained series, not repeated citation of one early paper |
| **Distribution of first/co-first authorship across students** | author lists | every student producing = training quality, which is what the user's own record depends on |
| Funding base and platform/technical posts | grant disclosures, unit pages, group composition | stable funding makes controllable intensity plausible |

## 2. Intensity — check the institution layer before the group layer

| Contract type | Baseline pressure | Evidence pattern |
|------|:--:|------|
| **Pre-tenure / "up-or-out"** (预聘—长聘) | highest | ~seven in ten double-first-class universities in China now run this system; the six-year clock dominates the postdoc's daily tempo |
| Post-tenure | medium-high, **not** safe | periodic review has been extended to tenured staff (multi-year cycles plus mid-term assessment); failing review can mean downgrade or pay cut |
| Permanent establishment post / technical-support post | lowest | public postings explicitly advertising no competitive elimination; also the lowest ceiling |

Then the group layer, by reliability of evidence:

| Reliability | Indicator |
|:--:|------|
| **high** | group size and structure (technical staff / platform posts absorb repetitive work); time-to-graduation history; whether the PI holds posts at several institutions or heavy administrative duties (their attention is split); lab access/hours rules |
| medium | submission cadence visible on the group page; meeting frequency and format |
| weak | anonymous forum reviews (heavy selection bias) |
| **the only reliable check** | ask **two** current or former members — and ask three fixed questions, never "are you busy?": daily hours actually spent in the lab; weekends/holidays; average time to graduation |

## 3. Thought-content — reject "more hands than heads" groups

Detection signals (all externally checkable before applying):

- **The job description is an execution checklist** — expression, purification, screening, passaging — with
  no mention of method development, design/modelling, or writing.
- **The group's output structure is monotone** — papers are all "activity of variant X of protein Y", with no
  methodology, tool, or computational work in the mix.
- **The postdoc's role is backfill** — the students' projects are already defined and the new postdoc
  inherits an existing experimental line.
- **No discussion culture** — lab meetings are progress reports rather than problem discussions; students
  do not raise critical or constructive points (this is the standard tell for over-managed groups).
- **High technical-repetition share** and no design step in the loop.
- **Authorship/writing rights are unclear** or the postdoc is not expected to draft papers — that directly
  caps the credit asset (A6).

Judgment rule to state plainly: **if 80% of the time is hands rather than head, the group is disqualified**
regardless of its publication record — it conflicts with any design-side or methodology-side goal.

Before applying, two mandatory actions: contact two group members with the three fixed questions, and read
the titles and author lists of the group's last five years of papers (monotone output and student-output
spread are visible from the list alone).

## 5. Keep a calibration yardstick

Record the user's own current group (and any group they name as a negative example) in the vault as a
reference point — a known "high output, low thought-content" group is what makes the abstract signals
above judgeable in one glance later.

## 6. Open presupposition to re-confirm, not assume

The academic-vs-industry choice for the first post-graduation job is a user decision that changes what
"accumulation" even means (papers and method vs platform and product). Re-ask it; do not smuggle it in as
settled because academic screening came up.
